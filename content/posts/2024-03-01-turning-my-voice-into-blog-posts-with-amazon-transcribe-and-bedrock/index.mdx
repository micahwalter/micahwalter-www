**Finding My Creative Voice**

I've always felt a tension between my technical side and my artistic side. For years, I've compartmentalized these interests, keeping my creative pursuits separate from my professional work in technology. Recently, however, I've started questioning that approach. I believe there is power in integrating all aspects of myself into one cohesive identity.

**Early Exploration at the Intersection of Art and Tech**

My first foray into blending technology and artistic creativity was with a 2015 project called Curatorial Poetry. While working at the Cooper Hewitt Smithsonian Design Museum, I created a system that automatically generated "poems" by remixing object descriptions from the museum's open API. The resulting poetic fragments highlighted the artistry in the original catalog descriptions. This project showed me the potential in using code to power creative expression.

**Revisiting Generative AI Art**

With the recent explosion in generative AI, I decided to revisit the intersection of technology and creativity. I'm currently building a system that uses a large language model to generate original poetry inspired by museum artifacts. Through livestreams and blog posts, I'm documenting the process of constructing the project on AWS services like Lambda and Bedrock. My goal is twofold: to experiment with cutting-edge AI capabilities, and to share technical knowledge with others.

**Finding My Unified Creative Voice**

While fun, my how-to AWS articles have catered to a narrow audience. In focusing so much on technical tips, I've lost sight of the bigger picture: expressing who I am as a creative builder. I aim to change that. Moving forward, I want to fully merge my artistic and engineering sides under one umbrella. I plan to infuse more creativity into my technical writing and connect my personal projects back to professional skills. Rather than keeping these worlds separate, I will integrate all my multifaceted interests into one unified voice.

At my core, I'm a builder who loves making things. Whether I'm crafting sculptures, designing APIs, or training neural networks, creation energizes me. By embracing this identity wholeheartedly, I hope to evolve a blog and brand that fully represents my passions. My creative output will encompass technology, yes, but also poetry, generative art, and whatever else inspires me. I don't need to compartmentalize anymore. I can simply create as my integrated self: builder, engineer, artist, and lifelong learner.

The above blog was written by [Anthropic's Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/product). It's not bad. It doesn't really sound like me (I'm not a sculptor). But, that's because it's not me. It's Claude! To generate this post, I recorded my voice with my iPhone, uploaded the audio file to [Amazon Simple Storage Service (S3)](https://aws.amazon.com/s3) and then used [Amazon Transcribe](https://aws.amazon.com/transcribe/) to turn the audio into text.

On it's own, Transcribe works great, but it's very much a word for word transcription of everything I said, including the "um" and "uh" filler words. The text is also more of a discussion vs. an actual blog post. So, I took that output from Transcribe and put it into [Amazon Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock) and asked Claude to convert it to something closer to a blog format.

Below is the raw input to Claude including my prompt asking Claude to re-imagine my voice recording as a blog.
